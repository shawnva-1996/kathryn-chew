**The short answer:** before you talk price with anyone, ask them one question, out loud and in writing: *who are you representing in this deal?* In one recent case, a salesperson stood in the middle of a sale where the sellers thought he was the buyer, the buyers thought he was the sellers' agent, and he quietly changed every offer that passed through him.

---

## A flyer in the letterbox

Mr and Mrs L found a flyer in their letterbox. A "Mr C" was looking to buy a home and could pay cash over valuation.

They had decided to sell their HDB flat themselves, without an agent. So they called the number, and a viewing with Mr C was arranged.

Meanwhile, a couple, the N's, were hunting for a resale flat. They didn't want an agent either; they wanted to save the commission. They saw a listing for a flat in the area and called the salesperson behind it, Mr K. He arranged a viewing.

Same day. Same time. Different flat from the one in his listing.

Mr K brought the N's to the L's home. The L's thought Mr K was "Mr C", the buyer from the flyer. The N's thought he was the sellers' agent. He let both of them keep believing it.

## Two stories, one gap

Here is what each side heard. Notice where the numbers don't meet.

| What actually happened | What Mr K told the other side |
|---|---|
| The N's offered **$680,000** | He told the sellers they offered **$660,000** |
| The L's said they'd accept **$685,000** | He told the buyers the minimum was **$690,000** |
| The N's offered **$690,000** | He told the sellers they offered **$685,000** |

The sellers accepted $685,000. The buyers believed they were paying $690,000.

The $5,000 in between? Mr K told the N's the sellers weren't paying him, so the buyers should. He had them sign an Estate Agency Agreement for a $5,000 commission, described as "paperwork for his fee." He didn't explain that signing it meant they were appointing him as *their* agent. He didn't give them copies of what they signed.

When HDB's resale portal asked the N's to have their salesperson submit the application, he told them to declare that they had no salesperson.

It all came out at completion, when the L's and the N's finally compared notes. Neither side had ever engaged him. The N's refused to pay the $5,000.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **The L's and the N's** | A whole sale negotiated on false numbers. The sale went through, and the buyers refused the $5,000 commission. |
| **Mr K, the salesperson** | Fined **$10,000, suspended 7 months**, plus $2,000 costs. He had a previous record for practising without a licence. |

The families here were fortunate. The deal closed, and the $5,000 was never paid. But think about how close it came, and how little either side could see.

## The lesson underneath

Both families made the same reasonable choice: *we'll do this ourselves and save on commission.* That is a legitimate way to buy and sell an HDB flat.

But going without an agent doesn't mean there's no one in the middle. It means you have to be the one who checks who is in the middle.

The person who carries the offers controls the story. If you never hear the other side directly, you are trusting that one person completely.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't assume you know someone's role.**
The sellers assumed "buyer." The buyers assumed "sellers' agent." Nobody asked. Ask on day one: *Who are you? Who are you representing? Who is paying you?*

**2. Don't negotiate only through someone you haven't verified.**
If you can't confirm who someone is, ask for offers in writing, signed, and talk to the other party directly before anything is final.

**3. Don't sign anything described as "just for my fee."**
An Estate Agency Agreement is the document that appoints an agent to act for you. Read it. If you don't want an agent, don't sign one.

**4. Don't leave without copies.**
You are entitled to copies of what you sign. If they aren't offered, ask on the spot.

**5. Don't make a false declaration because someone said "it makes no difference."**
If an HDB form asks whether you have a salesperson, answer truthfully.

## What to do instead

- **Look the person up** on CEA's Public Register before you meet. Check that the name, registration number and agency match.
- **Ask for the offer in writing** each time a number changes hands.
- **If something feels off, call the other party** or ask to meet with everyone present. It's your home and your money; this is not rude.
- **Be careful with flyers** promising cash over valuation. Some are marketing by salespeople rather than genuine buyers. Treat the person who calls as a salesperson until they show you otherwise.

## What would change this answer

This is one case, and most salespeople do not behave like this. If you are doing a DIY resale, HDB's own resale portal guides both parties through the steps, and you can ask HDB directly if you're unsure what a form is asking.

## Quick questions

**How do I check if someone is a registered property salesperson?**
Search their name or registration number on the Council for Estate Agencies' Public Register.

**Is a "cash buyer" flyer in my letterbox real?**
It may be, but in this case it was a salesperson using a made-up buyer identity. Verify who the person is before you discuss price.

**What is an Estate Agency Agreement?**
It's the agreement that appoints a salesperson to act for you and sets out their commission. Signing it creates a client relationship, so read it before you sign.

**What happened to the salesperson in this case?**
A CEA Disciplinary Committee fined him $10,000 and suspended him for 7 months, with $2,000 costs.
