**The short answer:** don't take the floor area from the listing. Check it against an official record, such as the HDB valuation report, the seller's HDB documents or an SLA property search, before you make an offer. One buyer only viewed a flat because the advertisement said 1,001 sq ft. It was actually about 958 sq ft, and she found out after she had already paid her option fee.

---

## A number that made the shortlist

Ms L was looking for a 4-room HDB flat of at least 1,000 sq ft. That was her rule.

A listing caught her eye: **93 sqm / 1,001 sq ft**, asking $418,000. It cleared her bar by one square foot. She called the seller's agent, Mr V, and arranged a viewing.

She liked it. She offered **$400,000**, and the sellers accepted. She paid a $1,000 option fee.

The HDB valuation came back at **$375,000**. That meant $25,000 in cash over valuation. Shocked by that number, Ms L and her husband went back to negotiate, and the price came down to **$390,000** under a fresh Option.

Then the second valuation report arrived. This time they noticed a line they had missed before: **89 sqm**.

They checked with HDB and the valuers. The flat was 89 sqm, about 958 sq ft, and that already included a recess area. Mr V had added a 4 sqm recess on top of the true area when he wrote the advertisement. The seller's own HDB paperwork had said 89 sqm all along.

Mr V told them the size difference was "already factored into" the lower price.

Ms L went ahead anyway. A baby was due in a few months, and she didn't want to start searching again. But she would never have viewed the flat if the listing had been right.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Ms L, the buyer** | Bought a flat about **43 sq ft smaller** than her minimum, after stress and two rounds of negotiation, with the clock ticking. |
| **Mr V, the sellers' agent** | Received **$180** as his share of commission. Fined **$3,000 and suspended 2 months**, plus $2,000 costs. |

The Disciplinary Committee found no dishonesty. Mr V had simply believed the wrong number and never checked it against the official one. That was enough.

## The moment that matters

The wrong number did its damage at the very start: at the shortlist.

By the time the truth surfaced, Ms L had viewed the flat, made an offer, paid a fee and planned her family's next few months around it. Walking away had become expensive in ways that had nothing to do with money.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't shortlist on the advertised floor area.**
Listings are written by people. The official record is what counts.

**2. Don't let a "just over the line" number go unchecked.**
1,001 sq ft against a 1,000 sq ft rule is exactly the kind of figure to verify first.

**3. Don't skim the valuation report.**
The floor area is printed on it. Ms L's family missed it the first time because the valuation shock took all their attention.

**4. Don't accept "it's already in the price" without the numbers.**
If the size is wrong, ask for the price per square foot on the true size, and decide again.

## What to do instead

Before you offer on a flat where size matters to you:

- **Ask the seller's side for the floor area from an official document,** such as the seller's HDB records, and ask whether it includes any recess or extension.
- **Check it yourself** with an official source, such as a property search on SLA's INLIS or HDB's records.
- **Compare price per square foot on the true size** against recent sales of the same flat type nearby.
- **Read the valuation report in full** when it arrives, not only the value.

## What would change this answer

How a flat's floor area is recorded can depend on approved extensions or recess areas. If anything doesn't match, ask HDB directly. Treat any size in a listing as a claim to verify, not a fact.

## Quick questions

**Where can I check an HDB flat's official floor area?**
From the seller's HDB documents, the HDB valuation report, or a property search through SLA's INLIS service.

**Is a recess area counted in the floor area?**
It depends on how it was bought and approved. In this case, the official 89 sqm already included the recess, and adding it again overstated the size.

**Who is responsible for the size in an advertisement?**
The salesperson who publishes it must verify it. In this case, the seller's agent was disciplined for not checking the official record.

**What if I find out after paying the option fee?**
Talk to the seller's side and your lawyer straight away. Your options shrink quickly once you have committed.
