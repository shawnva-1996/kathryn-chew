**The short answer:** if you sell your HDB flat and take a second HDB housing loan for the next one, HDB expects part of your cash proceeds to go into the new flat. Generally you keep the higher of $25,000 or half the cash; HDB counts the rest when it decides how much it will lend you. One couple heard about this rule only after their buyers had exercised the Option. They expected $75,000 in cash. They ended up borrowing from a moneylender to renovate.

---

## "You'll get about $75,000"

Mr and Mrs K owned an HDB flat bought with an HDB loan. A salesperson, Mr B, knocked on their door to ask if they would sell.

They told him their plan clearly. Sell this flat. Buy a 5-room resale flat with a second HDB loan. Use the cash to clear $30,000 to $40,000 of personal debts and renovate the new place.

Mr B gave them a number: at $490,000, they would walk away with about $45,516 in cash. With their debts, that wasn't enough, so they decided not to sell.

Then Mr B came back. He said the flat could fetch $520,000 to $530,000. They agreed to sell. They repeated their plan: a second HDB loan for the next flat.

An offer came in at $520,000. Mr B told them their cash proceeds would be **$75,000**. Pleased with the price, they accepted. They even agreed to pay him an extra $3,760 in commission on top of the original $10,015.20.

The buyers exercised the Option.

Three days later, Mr B mentioned the rule for the first time. Because they were taking a second HDB loan, a large part of that cash had to go into the next flat. By his own calculation, about $44,541 had to be set aside.

The K's asked whether the buyers would let them out of the deal and take their option money back. The buyers said no.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Mr and Mrs K, the sellers** | About **$30,459** less cash than they had been promised, roughly 6% of the sale price. To renovate, they took a **$22,000 personal loan** from a licensed moneylender, which cost **$29,680.50** with interest. |
| **Mr B, their agent** | Waived the extra $3,760 but kept his **$10,015.20** commission. Fined **$10,000 and suspended 6 months**; on appeal, reduced to **$8,000 and 5 months 2 weeks**, plus $2,000 costs. |

The Appeals Board made a point worth remembering. Mr B had more than 12 years of experience, and he admitted the rule had been in place since the day he started. He had several chances to mention it. He knew exactly what the couple needed the money for.

## The moment that matters

The number that persuaded the K's to sell was the cash figure. Not the price. The cash.

That is true for most sellers. You don't spend the sale price. You spend what lands in your bank account after the loan, the CPF refund and the rules have all taken their share. If that figure is wrong, the decision built on it is wrong too.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't agree to sell on a cash figure you haven't checked.**
Ask how it was worked out. Ask what it assumes about your next home.

**2. Don't assume "cash proceeds" means "cash I can spend".**
If you are taking a second HDB loan, part of it is already committed to your next flat.

**3. Don't tell your plan once and assume it was heard.**
The K's told Mr B three times. Ask your agent to write your plan down and show you how each rule affects it.

**4. Don't wait until after the Option to ask the hard questions.**
Once the buyer exercises the Option, you are committed. The questions that change your mind need answers before then.

## What to do instead

Before you grant an Option to Purchase:

- **Decide how you will finance the next home:** HDB loan, bank loan or cash. The rules on your proceeds depend on it.
- **Read HDB's page on HDB housing loans** and look for the part about cash proceeds from the flat you sold.
- **Ask for a written cash estimate** that shows the sale price, outstanding loan, CPF refund with accrued interest, any amount HDB expects you to put into the next flat, and what's left.
- **Plan your debts and renovation on the money that's truly left,** not on the headline figure.
- **If anything doesn't add up, ask HDB directly** before you sign.

## What would change this answer

HDB decides the amount for each case, and its rules change. Today HDB says you generally keep the higher of $25,000 or 50% of the cash proceeds, and HDB may decide differently depending on how the flat was owned or on a court order. If you finance the next flat with a bank loan instead, this particular rule does not apply in the same way, but others will. Please check HDB's current page and your own HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter.

## Quick questions

**Do I have to use my cash proceeds for my next HDB flat?**
If you take a second HDB housing loan, HDB generally expects the CPF refund and up to 50% of the cash proceeds from your last flat to go towards the new one. You generally keep the higher of $25,000 or 50% of the cash.

**Does this apply if I take a bank loan instead?**
This rule is part of HDB's housing loan terms. If you use a bank loan, different rules apply, so check with HDB and your bank.

**When should my agent tell me about this?**
Before you agree to sell. In this case the agent was disciplined for only mentioning it after the Option was exercised.

**Can I cancel the sale if I find out too late?**
Only if the buyer agrees. In this case, the buyers chose to go ahead.
