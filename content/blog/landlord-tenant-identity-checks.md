**The short answer:** before anyone moves into your home, the agent must check the tenant's original identity card or pass, match the photo to the person's face, and verify the document with the authorities. These checks are required, and they exist for landlords. In one case, a checklist said all three were done. None really were. The "tenant" was using a stranger's identity, the home was raided by police, and the landlord lost about **$5,200** in rent.

---

## Photos of a card, sent by WhatsApp

Mrs W wanted to rent out her private home. Her agent, Ms A, advertised it online.

A man replied on WhatsApp. He said he was a permanent resident and wanted the place for himself. He came to the viewing, and they agreed on **$2,800 a month** for a year.

Ms A asked for his identity card. He sent photos of the front and back, under another man's name. He said he had lost the card, made a police report, then found it again. Was it still valid? Ms A said it was fine, and she would check later.

She prepared the tenancy agreement. He signed it under the other man's name. He got the keys.

Ms A filled in the required checklist. It said she had seen the original card, matched the photo to his face and verified the card with the Immigration and Checkpoints Authority. Mrs W signed it too, trusting that it was true.

## What actually happened

The man on the card had been overseas the whole time. He had never lost his card, never seen the home and never heard of the lease. Someone had taken his identity.

The rent stopped. When Ms A and Mrs W's husband went in, the home was in disarray. Police had raided it days earlier. A woman staying there was arrested for vice-related offences and for overstaying.

The online check with ICA was done five months after the lease began, the day after that visit.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Mrs W, the landlord** | Lost about **$5,200** in rent: a month unpaid beyond the deposit, then $400 a month less from a replacement tenant. Plus the cost of cleaning and restoring her home. |
| **An innocent man** | Had his identity misused for a lease he knew nothing about. |
| **Ms A, the agent** | Received **$1,260** commission. Fined **$6,000 and suspended 7 months**, plus $2,000 costs. She had 15 years of experience. |

The Disciplinary Committee was direct. These checks are not paperwork. They exist for exactly the risks that happened here: false identities, unlawful occupants and misuse of the home. A checklist marked "done" when it wasn't gave Mrs W a false sense of safety.

## The moment that matters

The tenant offered a story about the card: lost, reported, found. That story was the moment to slow down, not speed up.

A checklist can only protect you if the boxes are true. When you sign it, ask to see the proof behind each tick.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't accept photos of an ID in place of the real card.**
Anyone can forward a picture. The check is on the original, in person.

**2. Don't hand over keys before the checks are complete.**
"I'll verify it later" means the tenant is already in your home.

**3. Don't sign the checklist on trust.**
Ask your agent to show you the verification result before you sign.

**4. Don't ignore a strange story.**
A lost-then-found card, an urgent move-in, or reluctance to meet in person are all reasons to check more carefully.

## What to do instead

Before your tenant moves in, ask your agent to show you:

- **The original identity card or pass,** seen in person, with a copy kept.
- **A face-to-face match** between the person and the photo.
- **The verification result** from ICA for citizens and permanent residents, or the right check for the pass holder's status for foreigners.
- **The same checks for every occupier,** not only the person signing the lease.
- **A signed checklist with the proof attached,** before the keys change hands.

## What would change this answer

The required checks differ for citizens, permanent residents and foreigners, and CEA updates its practice guidelines from time to time. HDB flats have their own extra rules on who may rent and for how long. Please check CEA's current guidelines and, for HDB flats, HDB's rules before you rent out.

## Quick questions

**What checks must an agent do on a tenant in Singapore?**
For Singapore citizens and permanent residents: check the original identity card, match the photo to the person face to face, and verify the card's validity with ICA, keeping a record. Different checks apply to foreigners.

**Can my agent accept a photo of the tenant's IC?**
Not in place of the original. The check is meant to be done on the original card, in person.

**What if I rent out my home without an agent?**
These rules bind agents, but the risks are yours. Do the same checks yourself.

**What happens if the tenant gave a false identity?**
In this case, the landlord lost rent and had her home misused. The agent was disciplined, but the penalty did not repay her.
