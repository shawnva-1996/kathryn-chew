**The short answer:** when you buy a home in Singapore, there is no GST on the price. When you buy a commercial or industrial property from a GST-registered seller, there usually is, and today that is 9% on top of the price. One buyer found out *after* she had signed. It cost her $43,400 that she never planned for.

---

## "This one is not subject to GST"

Ms W wanted an industrial unit as an investment. Buy it with a tenant already in place, collect the rent. Her budget was set, and she had one firm condition.

At the viewing, she said it plainly to the seller's agent, Ms Y: *I don't want to pay GST.*

Ms Y told her the sale was not subject to GST. When Ms W's first offer was turned down, Ms Y said it again, as part of explaining why the offer was too low.

Ms W's own agent, Mr H, was there for all of it. He did a title search. He checked the sellers for bankruptcy. He sent the paperwork to her lawyers. What he never did was check the one thing his client had said mattered most to her.

They agreed on $1.24 million. Ms W exercised the Option to Purchase.

A few days later, the sellers' lawyers raised it. GST was payable after all.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Ms W, the buyer** | Paid **$43,400 in GST** she had not budgeted for. The sellers would not share it. Neither agent paid her back. |
| **Mr H, her agent** | Earned **$6,200** commission on the deal. Fined **$7,000, suspended 5 months**, plus $2,000 costs. |

The Disciplinary Committee said something I keep coming back to. It wasn't only the extra $43,400. The *whole negotiation* had been built on a wrong fact. Every offer and counter-offer assumed a price without GST.

When the foundation is wrong, every number above it is wrong too.

## Why this catches people out

Most of us learn property on homes. And for homes, GST simply isn't part of the conversation: IRAS treats the sale of residential property as exempt.

So when someone moves from buying a condo to buying a factory unit or an office, they bring their home-buying instincts with them. The listing doesn't mention GST, so there must be none. That is the trap.

With non-residential property, whether GST applies depends on the **seller**: if the seller is GST-registered, GST is generally charged on the sale. You cannot tell that from the listing. You cannot tell it from how confident someone sounds.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't take "no GST" on someone's word.**
Ms Y said it twice. She was the seller's agent, and she turned out to be wrong. Confidence is not confirmation.

**2. Don't assume the listing tells you everything.**
The listing in this case said nothing about GST. Silence was not a "no".

**3. Don't let your agent check everything except the thing you care about.**
Title search, bankruptcy search, paperwork to the lawyers: all done. The one condition the buyer had said out loud: never checked. Tell your agent your deal-breakers, then ask them how they will verify each one.

**4. Don't negotiate the price before you know the full cost.**
On a $1.24 million property, 9% GST at today's rate would be $111,600. That is not a rounding error. It changes what the property is worth to you.

## What to do instead

Before you make any offer on a commercial or industrial property:

- **Ask, in writing:** is the seller GST-registered, and will GST be charged on this sale?
- **Get the answer confirmed by the seller's side in writing**, ideally through the lawyers, not just a conversation at the viewing.
- **Make sure the Option to Purchase states clearly** whether the price includes or excludes GST. Ask your lawyer to look at this specifically.
- **Run your numbers with GST included** if there is any doubt: your loan, your cash, your rental return.
- **If you're GST-registered yourself,** ask your accountant whether any of it can be claimed. That is a question for them, not for me.

## What would change this answer

GST treatment depends on the type of property, how it's approved for use, and the seller's GST status. Mixed-use properties can be partly taxable. Please check IRAS's guidance and speak to your lawyer or tax adviser for your own purchase.

## Quick questions

**Is there GST when I buy an HDB flat or a condo?**
No. IRAS treats the sale of residential property as exempt from GST.

**Is there GST when I buy a shop, office or industrial unit?**
Often, yes. If the seller is GST-registered, GST is generally charged on a non-residential sale. The rate has been 9% since 1 January 2024.

**Who should check whether GST applies?**
You, your agent and your lawyer, before you make an offer. In this case, the buyer's agent was disciplined for not taking adequate steps to verify it.

**Can I get the GST back from the agent if they told me wrong?**
In this case, neither agent compensated the buyer. A disciplinary penalty punishes the agent; it does not refund you.
