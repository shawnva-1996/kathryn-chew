**The short answer:** if you rent out bedrooms in your HDB flat, you must live in the flat yourself for the whole rental period, and you need HDB's approval first. Keeping a room "for yourself" that you only visit to clean does not count. One owner learned this the expensive way, and the agent who helped her was suspended.

---

## A new flat, a sensible-sounding plan

Picture Ms R. She has just collected the keys to her HDB flat. Her current lease elsewhere still has a few years to run, so she won't move in yet.

Two bedrooms are sitting empty.

So she has a plan that sounds tidy. Rent out both bedrooms. Keep the utility room for her own things. Pop by now and then to clean. When her other lease ends, move in properly.

She told her agent, Mr T, exactly that. He knew HDB's rule: when you rent out bedrooms, the owner has to live in the flat with the tenants. He didn't tell her. He listed the rooms instead.

Two sets of tenants moved in, about $950 a month each. A year later, Mr T helped renew both leases. Ms R still wasn't living there.

Then HDB came to inspect.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Ms R, the owner** | HDB told her it intended to **compulsorily acquire the flat**. After her appeal, HDB imposed a **$40,850 financial penalty** instead. One set of tenants had to leave early. |
| **Mr T, the agent** | **$11,000 penalty, 5 months' suspension**, plus $2,000 costs. He had earned $855 for each round of leases. |

Here is my rough maths from the dates in the record. If both rooms were let the whole time, the rent came to somewhere around $43,000 over about two years. The penalty was $40,850.

So two years of tenants, two years of keys and cleaning and messages, and almost nothing to show for it. And she came close to losing the flat itself.

## The moment that matters most

When HDB found out, Mr T offered a fix. He suggested Ms R tell HDB she had been staying in the utility room all along.

She didn't. She chose not to lie to HDB, and I respect that.

It's worth pausing here. When a plan starts to go wrong, the easiest-sounding way out is often the one that makes everything worse. A wrong arrangement can be corrected. A false statement to a government agency is a different kind of problem altogether.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't mistake "my agent didn't object" for "it's allowed."**
An agent saying yes is not the rule saying yes. Ms R told her agent the whole plan. His silence felt like permission. It wasn't.

**2. Don't treat "a room for me" as living there.**
Keeping belongings in a utility room and visiting to clean is storage, not residence. HDB's test is whether you actually live in the flat during the rental period.

**3. Don't renew on autopilot.**
The first lease was a mistake. The renewal was the same mistake, made again with a year's notice. Every renewal is a fresh moment to ask, "Is this still within the rules?"

**4. Don't let anyone talk you into misleading HDB.**
If someone suggests a story for HDB that isn't true, that is your signal to stop and get proper advice, not to follow it.

## What to do instead

- **Read HDB's own conditions** for renting out bedrooms before you list anything, and apply through HDB's e-Services for approval.
- **Ask yourself honestly:** will I be living here for the whole rental period? If the answer is no, bedroom rental is not your route.
- **If you can't move in yet,** look at whether you are eligible to rent out the *whole flat* (this has its own conditions, including your Minimum Occupation Period). If you aren't, the honest answer may be to leave the rooms empty for now. That costs something. It costs far less than $40,850.
- **Ask your agent to show you the rule,** not just tell you it's fine. A good agent will point you to the HDB page and walk through it with you.

## What would change this answer

HDB's rules change from time to time, and eligibility depends on your flat type, citizenship and when you bought. This story is about the bedroom-rental rule as it applied in this case. Please check HDB's current pages for your own flat before you decide anything.

## Quick questions

**Can I rent out my HDB bedrooms if I don't live in the flat?**
No. For bedroom rental, HDB requires the owner to continue living in the flat during the rental period, and the rental needs HDB's approval.

**Does keeping a room for my own use count as living there?**
Not on its own. In this case, keeping belongings in the utility room and visiting to clean did not count.

**What can happen if HDB finds out?**
In this case, HDB moved to compulsorily acquire the flat, and settled on a $40,850 penalty after the owner appealed. Outcomes depend on the facts.

**Is it only the owner who gets into trouble?**
No. The agent who facilitated the leases was fined $11,000 and suspended for 5 months by a CEA Disciplinary Committee.
