**The short answer:** when you sell, the money pays off your housing loan first. What's left goes back to your CPF accounts. If that isn't enough to return everything you used plus accrued interest, you have a "negative sale". If you sold at market value, you usually don't have to top up the gap in cash, but your CPF only gets back what the sale actually covered. One couple was told their CPF would be refunded in full. It came back **$57,972.24** short, and they found out only when they tried to use it for their next home.

---

## The answer that was half right

Mr and Mrs T asked Ms G, their salesperson, to sell their HDB flat. She worked out that unless it sold for at least $522,000, it would be a negative sale.

Ms G hadn't handled one before, so she called the CPF Board. She was told that if the flat sold at or above market valuation, the sellers would not need to top up the shortfall in cash.

That much was true. But she passed it on as something bigger: no top-up, *and* your CPF will be fully refunded on completion. She didn't check what a negative sale would actually do to the refund.

The T's dropped their minimum to $500,000. Then they agreed to sell at **$480,000**. Ms G's updated sums showed the cash side would be about $60,000 short, but that their CPF would get back **$287,000** combined. She repeated that only a sale below valuation would need a cash top-up, and their CPF would come back in full.

The valuation came in at $455,000. The sale was above it. The T's felt safe.

HDB's letter said the sale would be **$78,933.55** negative. The CPF refunds actually paid came to **$229,027.76**. Still trusting the advice, the T's didn't notice the difference, and committed to buying a private home.

Months later, when they went to use their CPF for that purchase, the money wasn't there. The shortfall had been taken from what would have gone back to their CPF, in proportion.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Mr and Mrs T, the sellers** | Their CPF got back **$57,972.24 less** than they had been told, about 12.1% of the sale price. Already committed to the next purchase, they covered the gap from their remaining CPF and in cash. They said they would not have sold, or bought, had they known. |
| **Ms G, their agent** | Received **$9,345.79** commission and offered no compensation. Fined **$6,500 and suspended 3 months**, plus $2,000 costs. |

## The moment that matters

Ms G asked the right organisation. She just stopped at the first half of the answer.

"You don't need to top up in cash" answers one question. "How much will my CPF actually get back?" is a different question. The T's needed the second answer, because their next home depended on it.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't treat "no cash top-up" as "no loss".**
The loss is still there. It just shows up in your CPF accounts instead of your bank account.

**2. Don't commit to the next home before you see HDB's figures.**
HDB sends a statement of estimated sale proceeds. Read it line by line against what you were told.

**3. Don't let a big number go unchecked.**
The T's received a refund letter that was tens of thousands short of the estimate. Ask "why is this different?" the day the letter arrives.

**4. Don't rely on a salesperson's first negative sale.**
If your agent says it's new to them, that's honest. Then go together to the official calculators and to HDB or CPF for written confirmation.

## What to do instead

If your sale might not cover your loan and CPF:

- **Get your outstanding loan and your CPF used plus accrued interest** from HDB and the CPF website. These are the two numbers that decide everything.
- **Work out the gap at several prices,** and ask what your CPF would actually receive at each one.
- **Check whether the price is at or above market valuation,** because that affects whether you need to top up in cash.
- **Wait for HDB's estimate of sale proceeds** before you commit to buying your next home, and compare it against your own numbers.
- **Plan the next purchase on the CPF you will really have,** not on the CPF you used.

## What would change this answer

CPF's rules on refunds, top-ups and accrued interest are set by the CPF Board and can change. Selling below market value, owning with others, or using CPF from several members all affect the result. Please check CPF's and HDB's current guidance for your own sale, and get the figures in writing.

## Quick questions

**What is a negative sale?**
When the sale price isn't enough to repay your outstanding housing loan and refund all the CPF you used plus accrued interest.

**Do I have to top up my CPF in cash after a negative sale?**
CPF says you generally don't need to top up the shortfall in cash if the property is sold at market value. Selling below market value may mean you do.

**Will I get all my CPF back?**
Not in a negative sale. Your CPF gets back what's left after the loan is repaid, which is less than what you used plus accrued interest.

**Where do I see the actual figures?**
In HDB's statement of estimated sale proceeds and the refund notices that follow. Check them before you commit to your next home.
