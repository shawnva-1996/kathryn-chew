**The short answer:** if you sell a home within the Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD) holding period, you pay a percentage of the price to IRAS. For homes bought on or after 4 July 2025, that period is four years. Before you grant an Option, work out the exact date your holding period ends, and the duty if you sell before it. One seller missed the line by **22 days** and paid **$11,000**.

---

## "A waiting game"

Ms R needed to sell her HDB flat early, for personal reasons, and got HDB's approval to do so. Her salesperson, Ms S, put it on the market.

Ms R told Ms S plainly: she wanted to lose as little as possible.

Ms S mentioned a "potential SSD". Ms R asked what that was. HDB had only told her she *might* have to pay it. Ms S never explained what it was, or how much it would be.

Instead, Ms S said the flat would reach its third year of occupancy only in March, months away, and that it was "really like a waiting game" because prices might fall further. Ms R understood that waiting to avoid SSD would mean months of falling prices.

That wasn't true. The flat would pass its holding period in **less than four weeks**.

Then Ms S added: worst case, they could appeal to their MP to waive it. *"We dun alert IRAS."*

Ms R granted the Option. The sale price was **$275,000**. The buyer exercised it.

The flat crossed its holding period 22 days after the Option was granted.

## What it cost

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Ms R, the seller** | Paid **$11,000 SSD**, 4% of the price. IRAS rejected her waiver request. |
| **Ms S, her agent** | Received **$1,156.54** commission and made no restitution. Fined **$6,000 and suspended 3 months**, plus $2,000 costs. |

## The moment that matters

One number would have changed everything: the date the holding period ended.

Ms R could have waited three weeks and paid nothing. She didn't wait, because she was told the wait was months long, and that the duty could probably be waived anyway.

## What to avoid

**1. Don't sell without the exact end date of your holding period.**
Not "around March". The actual date.

**2. Don't let anyone tell you SSD can be "appealed away".**
SSD waivers are only for exceptional circumstances, and appeals go to IRAS, not to an MP.

**3. Don't confuse different clocks.**
Occupancy years, the HDB Minimum Occupation Period and the SSD holding period are separate rules with separate dates.

**4. Don't weigh "prices might fall" against a duty you haven't calculated.**
Compare the real duty in dollars with a realistic price change over the actual waiting time.

## What to do instead

Before you grant an Option on a home you've owned for less than four years:

- **Find your date of purchase** as IRAS counts it, and work out when the holding period ends.
- **Check the SSD rate** for your holding period on IRAS's page.
- **Calculate the duty in dollars** at your expected sale price.
- **Compare it with waiting:** how many days or weeks until the duty drops or disappears?
- **Ask your agent to put the date and the duty in writing** before you decide.

## Today's SSD rates

For residential property bought on or after **4 July 2025**:

| Held for | SSD |
|---|---|
| Up to 1 year | 16% |
| More than 1 to 2 years | 12% |
| More than 2 to 3 years | 8% |
| More than 3 to 4 years | 4% |
| More than 4 years | None |

For property bought from 11 March 2017 to 3 July 2025, the holding period is three years, at 12%, 8% and 4%.

## What would change this answer

SSD rules depend on when you bought, and IRAS's rules on how the holding period is counted. Most HDB owners can't sell within four years anyway because of the Minimum Occupation Period, so SSD mostly matters for private homes, and for HDB flats sold early with HDB's approval, as in this case. Check IRAS's current page for your own dates.

## Quick questions

**How long is the SSD holding period now?**
Four years for residential property bought on or after 4 July 2025. It was three years for property bought from 11 March 2017 to 3 July 2025.

**Does SSD apply to HDB flats?**
It can. Most HDB flats can't be sold within the holding period because of the Minimum Occupation Period, but a flat sold early with HDB's approval may attract SSD.

**Can SSD be waived?**
Only in exceptional circumstances, and the request goes to IRAS. In this case, the waiver was refused.

**Who should work out my SSD?**
Your agent should tell you if it applies and how much. Check the dates yourself on IRAS's page too.
